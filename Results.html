Forward

Results on the poster were curated to improve readability. The bar-charts displaying the variation in seedling counts were filtered to remove the exceptionally high counts. We have an ongoing problem of imputing high seedling counts. This effects very few plots, but it makes the graphs hard to read. Each ecoregion’s results will display the graphs presented on the poster followed by the raw bar-chart, a plot that includes more species and a brief explanation. Also worthy of note, these graphs were made using the holdout data from the yaImpute function. They do not, as of now, show the observed data from our holdout set of test data. I will try to include the test set once I have everything in place.

Please recommend other charts/Results to show.

Below is a map showing the approximate FIA plot locations within the six ecoregions for which we are developing models. The drop down box in the upper right, labelled “On this page”, will quickly navigate between the map and each ecoregion’s results. It can be seen in the map that there are very few plots in the Columbia Basin and relatively few in Puget Trough. The small number of plots in these regions result in extremely variable seedling counts. For this reason, the Columbia Basin graphs were left out of the poster. Currently, we do not have plans for dealing with this. Thoughts or suggestions would be appreciated and contact info can be found on the Home tab.

North Cascades

Presence of Regeneration

These charts only show data for the 408 plots that yaImpute withheld for testing. Of these plots in the holdout set, about 325 had at least one seedling (Total) observed. Slightly fewer were imputed to have at least one of any species. In general this model appears to well at detecting if regeneration should be imputed.

We can see that most species are present in very few plots. There were about 11 species with appreciably large representations in the data.

Variation, Most Prevalent species

The graph on the right shows the same set most prevalent species as that on the left, but without filtering the extreme values. The 4 plots with counts exceeding 20,000 Trees per Acre were removed were removed to show variability in the majority of the data.

At least in the major species, variability appears fairly consistent in this model. There is some concern regarding the outliers, but for now, this seems promosing.

Variation, All species

Again, these plots show variability in seedling counts, but this time for all species. The plot on the right is mostly useless. It is displayed mostly for the purpose of full disclosure regarding the outliers. The plot on the left is the same, except the 4 high TPA plots were filtered out.

The plot on the left shows somewhat mixed results. There are a few that appear to have relatively close distributions. However PICO, Lodgepole Pine, stands out with largest discrepancy between Imputed and Observed data. From Lodgepole Pine, and to the right, it can be seen that these models depend on a sufficiently large number of sample plots to make accurate predictions. In the data input into the model, Lodgepole was present on 108 plots. There were 38 reserved for testing, so 70 were used to train the model.

If the reader has any suggestions on how to deal with these data limitations, I would be very happy to hear from you.
